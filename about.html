My approach

Simple on the screen, honest underneath.

Most of my work starts with something that looks simple on the screen but isn't underneath. A name change touches systems across departments. An AI answer is only as trustworthy as the content behind it. A pickup date depends on rules tied to every address.

I like to look beyond the interface, mapping the people, systems, and policies involved, so the experience can feel simple without pretending the complexity isn't there.

Accessibility and inclusion shape my decisions from the start, not at the end. Designing for a city as diverse as Boston, in multiple languages, across devices, and for people with very different levels of comfort with technology, has taught me that good design removes barriers and helps people feel confident and in control.

How I work

Four habits that show up in every project.

  • Map the system before designing the screen

    Workflow maps and service blueprints show where an experience really breaks, and give product, engineering, and operations a shared picture.

  • Design for different realities

    When systems behave differently for different people, I design paths that are honest about it instead of forcing one flow on everyone.

  • Bring partners in early in the flow

    HR, IT, content, and operational teams know where the hard problems are. Involving them early leads to designs that can actually ship.

  • Test with real people, then fix what they find

    Usability testing turns assumptions into evidence, and every finding gets an answer before launch.
